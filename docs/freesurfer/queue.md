# FreeSurfer 8.2.0: the queue

**Closed to FreeSurfer clean-room implementers** (it names FreeSurfer's files and functions;
`docs/freesurfer/README.md`). A clean-room implementer gets its item through its brief.

The order of work for FreeSurfer's port ([the plan](../freesurfer-plan.md), "Working modes"):
written by the inventory (relay 6), kept by the orchestrator. The waves, the modules and the
crates are explained in [waves.md](waves.md); each program's facts are on its card
(`cards/<program>.md`). The tables below are generated by `waves.py` (waves.md §7); the
orchestrator marks an item's progress in its brief and in NOTES, not by editing a generated row.
Brought in line with the user's decisions of 2026-11-12 by relay 8 (the placement table, W1 to W6:
waves.md §6), and with the orchestrator's decision on 3.1-25 (2026-11-13, by track A's relay 8 of the
foundations): the MRI structure is an item of its own before the readers that build it (3.1-55,
`fs.mri`, the crate `larmorx-freesurfer-mri` below `fs.io`); DICOM input moved to the completeness
items (`fs.dicomread`, 3.7-14 and 3.7-15, with the option paths into it, 3.7-16) after a W3 item for
CTN's element reading (3.1-56); the format probe's pieces (`IsDICOM`, `IsSiemensDICOM`, Bruker's
check) stay in `fs.io`, with `mri_identify`; 3.1-31, 3.1-32 and 3.1-41 retired (the modules they
ended are shorter now); the calls that still go against the crates' layers: waves.md §4.

## How to take an item

- **Numbers are names, kept stable** (since relay 8, [queue-ids.toml](queue-ids.toml)): an item
  keeps its number while it exists; a new item takes the next free number of its wave (so 3.1-53
  and 3.1-54 come after 3.1-52, and 3.2-41, the main package's plumbing, is the first row of 3.2);
  a number whose item is gone is retired, never given again (3.1-13 and 3.1-17, regrouped by W3;
  C3.6-06 and C3.6-07, now their programs' own clean-room items). The order of work is the order
  of the rows.
- **One item, one relay.** An item is bounded: one module's files (or a piece of them), one
  program, or one piece of a large program, at most about **2,500 lines** of FreeSurfer's function
  bodies (the budget is an assumption: calibrate it on the first three items). *Lines* are what
  the item brings: the functions only it reaches and its own files, counted once in the whole
  queue (static reach, so upper bounds: waves.md §3).
- **Kinds.** `replica` (a module of `crates-freesurfer/`) and a program's kind (`c++`, `tcsh`,
  `python-network`, ...): a **replica item**, whose author is also the **spec writer** of what it
  ports (`specs/freesurfer-<module>.md` or `specs/<program>.md`; one reading of the source, two
  products); `permissive`: a third-party piece ported from its own upstream into the main package
  (no FreeSurfer source); `W3 parser`: a pure-Rust parser in a permissive parser's place, with the
  same values and the same failure messages on every path `recon-all` and fMRIPrep can reach,
  proved by differential tests against the original in the unit oracle (no port: the original's
  lines are the behaviour it gives; W3, waves.md §6); `permissive (exact writer)`: a writer ported
  exactly, because its number formatting decides the output's bytes; `gpl`: a GPL piece whose
  replica lives alone in `larmorx-gpl` (`crates-gpl/`), written by a replica author there, which no
  FreeSurfer program links (GSL's generators, P-08); `spec + clean room`: a spec writer, then a
  clean-room implementer (never an agent of the replica or permissive tracks); `clean room only`:
  no replica, by the user's decision (Washington University's Talairach tools, P-12:
  `talairach_avi`, `mpr2mni305` and the 4dfp programs); `completeness`: the option paths of earlier
  programs into a module just brought; `plumbing`, `plumbing (main package)`, `oracle`,
  `inventory + spec`: what their row says. A replica's crate outside `crates-freesurfer/` is named
  in its row (`larmorx-gpl-gsl`; `tkregister2_cmdl`'s `larmorx-nc-tkregister2`, its own program in
  the `larmorx-nc` wheel).
- **Every program item, in full** (the plan, "Each program's validation"): every option, message,
  exit status and failure case with D13's switches; its spec; the replay on every recorded head in
  both streams (its *Checks*); synthetic edge cases for every option and failure; golden cases
  (volumes up to 32³, small synthetic surfaces); its defects decided by rules A to D (FreeSurfer's
  defect IDs from the orchestrator's range); its record; memory near the original's; its pages.
  *Checks* names what the recordings give it: "replay: N recorded steps in R runs" (the program is
  a recorded step), "through ...'s recorded steps" (it runs inside a script's step: the replay
  runs the script's step, or the program with the arguments the script's log shows), or
  fMRIPrep's and sMRIPrep's calls on the recorded subjects (programs.md §5; new runs through the
  oracle-output cache at one thread).
- **Inputs** names the modules the item uses (they come before it), the programs a script runs,
  the data files the program reads (data-files.toml) and what it *waits for* (a later module its
  option paths reach: completed by that wave's "option paths" item).
- **Side by side.** In a normal week one agent takes the next item. In a burst: items of different
  modules or programs (never two agents on one module); the N track beside 3.2-3.5 once 3.1's
  formats exist; **the clean rooms one wave behind** (the table after each wave's: the clean rooms
  of the previous wave, from the specs its replica items wrote, by agents who never read
  FreeSurfer's source).
- **Who may take what** (CLAUDE.md rule 1, "Roles"): replica and `gpl` items by replica authors
  (who may read FreeSurfer's source and the piece's), `permissive` and `W3 parser` items by the
  permissive track (from the piece's own upstream; FreeSurfer's call sites only), `spec + clean
  room` items by a spec writer and then a clean-room implementer, the clean rooms (C-items, CP-items)
  by agents that never read FreeSurfer's material: their crates are created by the first of them
  (C3.1-00), never by 3.1-01. The pieces whose copied form states no licence (Burkardt's 2006
  routines, 3.1-54) are built and published like the rest (the user, 2026-11-12); when the user
  decides on publishing, the orchestrator lists them.
- **The permissive pieces' clean rooms** (CP-01 to CP-13, the last table) come with the roadmap's
  step 6, the permissive ports' clean rooms in one block (next-steps, "The order from here"), not
  one wave behind.
- **Before 3.1:** the preparation's sub-tasks (done but its handoff: NOTES of the preparation part).
